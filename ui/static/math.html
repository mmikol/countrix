The Counter Utility Matrix

Countrix is short for Counter Utility Matrix, the equation below. The counter is who answers whom. The utility is what a six is worth once the facts are counted: one number for the whole six, not a sum of its picks' worths, since a pair can pay only together. The matrix is every row of the data crossed with every other: heroes against heroes, heroes against maps, both against the meta. A comp is the argmax, over the legal sixes, of the utility the matrix feeds.

DATA           = HEROES ∪ MAPS ∪ META
for each domain D in { HEROES, MAPS, META }:
  INDEPENDENT(D) = ⋃ facts(s)      over each row s of D
  DEPENDENT(D)   = ⋃ facts(s ⋈ t)  over the rows t that s joins, in D or beyond
  FACTS(D)       = INDEPENDENT(D) ∪ DEPENDENT(D)
FACTS          = FACTS(HEROES) ∪ FACTS(MAPS) ∪ FACTS(META)
FACTS(D) ∩ FACTS(E) = the joins of D with E, read as facts, for D ≠ E
STRATEGIES     = CONSTRAINTS ∪ HEURISTICS ∪ ASSUMPTIONS
COMP           = ARGMAX[ STRATEGIES( FACTS ) ]
               = the first six x of LEGAL by score(x)
LEGAL          = the sixes of released, unbanned heroes that keep the locked picks,
                 at most %(MAX_TANKS)d tanks, every CONSTRAINT true on FACTS(x)
score(x)       = base(x) + each HEURISTIC's term on FACTS(x); an ASSUMPTION adds nothing
FACTS(x)       = the board's FACTS, and the six's own, team and matchup, read on x
first          = the highest score to %(SCORE_PLACES)d places, then the board's draw, then the names

DATA: the tables as pulled. HEROES: kits, their published numbers, playstyles, counters, synergies. MAPS: mode, stages, sides, and the terrain the wiki's articles describe, counted as mentions per thousand words (chokes, interiors, high ground, flanks, sightlines, open ground, hazards, cover). META: win, pick and ban rates per hero and per map, dated.

FACTS: what the fact engine derives for one board and a six on it: the board's facts hold still, and the six's own, team and matchup, are read afresh on every six the function scores. The independent facts of a domain come from one row: a hero's pool, range and rates; a map's mode, stages and terrain, each feature z-scored across the maps with text; the meta's vintage.

The dependent facts are joins (⋈). A hero's rate on this map is heroes ⋈ map_meta ⋈ maps. The style a map rewards adds the rates' lift to the terrain's lean: lift is playstyle ⋈ map_meta, a style's heroes' win rate there over their own, z-scored across the maps; lean reads brawl from chokes and interiors, dive from high ground, flanks and hazards, poke from sightlines and open ground, then averages and z-scores across the maps with text. The rest are who a hero answers (heroes ⋈ counters ⋈ heroes), who it pairs with, the six aggregated, the twelve compared, and a ban weighed against both teams' counters. Facts are numbered F1, F2, …; every claim on the board cites one.

STRATEGIES: the playbook, one markdown file each. Constraints cut the space, then heuristics weigh what is left. A constraint is a limit: it always holds and is never weighted. A heuristic is a metric, a direction and a weight, or a bonus less a penalty times its weight; either may count only under a condition, its when. An assumption is prose: shown, never scored. Read as a function, STRATEGIES( FACTS ) is score(x) on the legal sixes: the constraints set its domain, the heuristics its terms, over the default engine's base(x), whose weights meta.md holds.

COMP: of every legal six, the first in the board's order: the highest score to %(SCORE_PLACES)d decimal places, then the board's tie-break draw, then the names, so it falls short of the highest score by less than a unit in the last of those places. The same board gives the same six.

How a six is chosen

Inference on maxims and facts. The maxims are the playbook's rules, the facts the database's numbers about this board, and together they make one objective, weighted and constrained, which the search solves exactly or refuses. Five steps, in order:

1  the space       every six of released, unbanned heroes that keeps the locked picks,
                   each set once, grouped tank, damage, support; at most %(MAX_TANKS)d tanks
2  the limits      each constraint removes the sixes that break it, and weighs nothing
3  the meta        the default engine scores every six left, times the meta weight:
                   win-rate edge trusted by pick rate, synergies, counters
4  the heuristics  each adds or subtracts, times its weight, from the same facts
5  the argmax      the best legal six, proved, then the next best in order;
                   every reason cites a fact

An open board held about 17.2 million legal sixes under the shipped playbook at the roster of 2026-09-28 (what is searched), and a ban or a locked pick leaves fewer. A synergy cell no hero's article writes is unknown, not zero: it reads the share of the written cells that claim their pair, so a pair neither article writes reads twice that, the written pairs' mean with their own blank cells read the same way. The weights are not learned: a rule's starting weight comes from its prose on the house scale, 0.25 a whisper, 1 the default, 2.5 strong and 4 dominant, or from the owner's word, as heal-rate's 2 did, and each rule's weight and each of the engine's is yours to turn - the tune tool for the file, a slider for a session, the Meta slider for the whole engine. A score is not a probability: a badge places it on the seat's span, from its floor, 0, to its optimal, 100, and the fight odds split 100 between the two seats' shares. The rates are Competitive Role Queue, 5v5, a proxy for Open Queue 6v6, so a value a hero has only beside a second tank, such as Zarya's in a two-tank front line, is missing from them.

The function: STRATEGIES( FACTS )

One six in, one number out: score(x), the default engine's base(x) with the playbook's heuristics on top; the constraints decide which sixes it is read on, and the assumptions add nothing. The facts are its namespace: team, enemy, matchup, map and world, the facts tab's own numbers, team and matchup read on the six.

The default engine scores every six before the playbook reads it, whatever the playbook holds: the six's win rates on this map, the wiki's synergy pairs among its picks, a cell no hero's article writes counted at the share of the written cells that claim, and the counter graph between it and the other side. A pick's win rate is read as its edge over a coin flip, trusted by its pick rate, so a hero picked in few matches counts for less. The counter graph is the wiki's edges, its Match-Up tables' and its Strategy sections', and where the wiki has no edge either way, the answers the kit derives: %(MECHANISMS)d mechanisms read off the two heroes' kits, from hitscan against a flier to anti-heal against self-healing, score every ordered pair, and of each loser's %(TOP_ANSWERS)d best answers - a score of %(THRESHOLD)g or more, and above the reverse pair's - those on a pair the wiki leaves out count; the others are not replaced. A wiki edge counts %(WIKI_WEIGHT)d, a derived one %(DERIVED_WEIGHT)d, and the board names every derived edge it counts with the mechanism that fired. Only this term reads a derived edge - the playbook's counter metrics read the wiki's graph alone.

The other side is its locked picks, or its likely six on this map until it locks one. On the board, blue's seat is solved against red's likely six as though red had picked it, so there every term reads it - the healing floor, the enemy.* and matchup.* metrics, the counter metrics and the scale's field. Where the other side has no picks and none are handed in - red's seat before blue picks, and infer before red reveals - only this term reads the likely six, and the rest read an empty side. A playbook of assumptions alone gets the six these three favour, scored and explained, and every other term sits on top of them.

A limit that fails removes the six, and no limit scores. A heuristic on a metric normalises it to 0..1 against %(REFERENCE_SIZE)s random legal sixes for the board, the reference sample, and against the board's field, flips it when minimising, then multiplies by its weight. The sample is drawn a legal shape first, each shape as likely, then each role's heroes, from every released hero, by a string seed of the map and the side alone: red, the bans and the locks never move it. The field is every legal six of each role's %(SCALE_POOL)d released heroes the board ranks first by a prior - the win rate here, 3 points for each of the other side's picks the hero answers less 3 for each that answers it, a point for the map's style and one for a best map - which reads the other side, never the bans or the locks. Both are measured on the whole map, and a six a limit refuses is left out of both.

A heuristic on a metric whose when the six decides - one that reads team.* or matchup.* - is a need: it counts only on a six in that state; met in full it costs nothing, unmet it costs s_n times its weight, its weight at most, so entering the guarded state never pays. A scored heuristic is never a need: it adds its weight times bonus minus penalty while its when holds, on the board or on the six, so a guard on the six's state can pay; a rule that should cost rather than forbid is one, when: not (the rule) with its penalty. An assumption adds nothing.

for one six x, with every limit holding:

The base's weights. They are the playbook's, not the code's: meta.md beside the strategy files holds them, and the tune tool changes them. The meta, %(W_META)g, scales the whole engine, and at 0 the playbook scores alone. Under it the rate term is in win-rate points and weighs %(W_RATE)g. The synergy and counter weights are set so that each term's spread - its highest less its lowest over a board's reference sample, the other side red's likely six - has a median over the maps about half the rate term's; synergy was set again once a blank synergy cell came to read the written cells' claim share. The calibration is in inference/base.py. The figures that follow are at meta 1 and the shipped playbook's weights, each spread its median over the maps; under another playbook the weights above are its meta.md's, and the figures do not carry over. A heuristic on a metric moves a six by its weight at most, since its norm is 0..1 and s_n is 1 at most. A scored one moves it by its weight times its bonus less its penalty, which its own expressions bound: the shipped control-points-have-edges by up to 1.5 times its weight, the other shipped scored rules by their weight at most. A base term has no such bound. A heuristic at weight 1 moves a six less than any base term spreads the sixes, at 2 about as far as the synergy or the counter term, and at the slider's %(WEIGHT_MAX)g further than the three together.

Sustained healing. A hero's hps is what it lands on teammates a second, summed over every piece that runs beside its weapon and over every teammate each piece reaches; its own healing is out. A beam that spends energy counts what the energy sustains: fire, wait out the regen delay, regenerate, at the cycle that heals most. An area heal counts the teammates it reaches. The six stand spread uniformly over a disk of radius R = %(FORMATION_RADIUS)g m, the shortest single-target heal range among the supports, and p(r) is the chance two of them stand within r:

The healing floor. The shipped playbook's healing heuristic, heal-rate, scored, charges a six that heals less than the other side's rate. It comes from a race. A six has a pool P (health, shield, armor), damage D and sustained healing onto teammates H, an area heal over the teammates it reaches and a beam at what its resource sustains. Each side's damage leaves the same share k of the other's healing landing, 0 < k ≤ 1, so x loses (D_r − k H_x) / P_x of its pool a second and red (D_x − k H_r) / P_r, and the one that loses the smaller share outlasts the other. The margin, (D_x / P_r − D_r / P_x) + k (H_x / P_x − H_r / P_r), splits into a damage half and a healing half, and the healing half breaks even when blue heals the same share of its own pool as red heals of its own, whatever k is. The need is that share of blue's pool, floored at red's healing in full. The floor is a choice, not the race's: parity asks less of a smaller pool, while the race rewards a larger one as long as a six loses health, so a six under red's pool cannot lower its bar by shedding pool; over red's pool the need still grows with the pool.

need(x)      = max( H_r, H_r / P_r · P_x )
shortfall(x) = max( 0, need(x) − H_x ) / need(x)      0 when need(x) is 0
charge(x)    = w · shortfall(x)      w the rule's weight in the playbook

H_x, P_x     x's summed sustained healing onto teammates and its pool, a form's armor in
H_r          red's healing  + f · d_support · hps_bench / 2
P_r          red's pool     + f · Σ_q d_q · median pool of role q
d_q          max( 0, 2 − red's picks in role q ): what the two-two-two still misses
f            red's open slots / Σ_q d_q, 0 when nothing is missing

Red here is the other side as the seat reads it. On the board, until red locks a pick, blue's seat reads red's likely six as red's picks, so the bar is that six's healing on its pool, and the map's pick rates and the synergy table choose its heroes; from red's first pick the open slots are filled as above, so the bar moves when red locks a hero its likely six already held. With red empty - red's seat before blue picks, or infer before red reveals - it is a two-two-two of role-median heroes: it heals world.hps_bench, twice the median support, on twice the sum of the role-median pools in the kit in force. A six must then heal that share of its own pool a second, and never less than world.hps_bench, and everything in that threshold is kit data. The sources give no absolute bar - no fight length, no ultimate charge - so the rule asks for parity with the other side's healing and no more; past it the race is the damage half's. The bar inherits three limits, read on the 6v6 kit as of 2026-09-26. The bench sits under a gap: Illari, Mizuki and Moira sit inside 0.7 hp/s of each other at the median, each on a judgement, the bench moves hp for hp with the two that hold the median, and one of them past Kiriko lifts it to about 151 hp/s. Overheal is out, so an untargeted area heal, which lands on full-health teammates too, reads above what it heals. Brigitte holds her Flail in contact throughout; at half contact she reads 61.7 hp/s in place of 98.4.

What 100 means. Each seat's best six on this board is its optimal, 100. Its 0 is the seat's floor: the lowest score among the sixes of its reference sample that keep the board's limits, scored on the board's stage. The sample is drawn from every released hero whatever the bans, so the floor six may hold a banned one; the field does not enter the floor, and 0 stands in where no six of the sample keeps the limits. A score is signed - the rate term counts each pick's edge over a coin flip - so zero anchors nothing, and a six below it would read 0 against any six above it. The badge above a picker places the seat's six on the span between the two - its picks at six, the best six its picks reach while it drafts, the optimal's 100 before any pick - under the default engine and this playbook, not a win probability:

share(x) = round( clamp( (score(x) − floor) / (best − floor), 0, 1 ) · 100 )
           best: the seat's optimal six; floor: the lowest score in its reference sample
           round: to the nearest whole number, a half to the even one

An optimal no higher than the floor leaves no share to divide, so every other comp reads unscored, and the reason is on hover. A comp whose own picks break a limit - a full six that breaks one, or picks no six can complete within the limits - is not allowed: no score, no share and no odds, and the badge names the limits its picks break or, where they break none as they stand, says that no six keeping them meets the limits. Red's picks are the other side's, and never ruled out.

Fight odds. Blue's share over the sum of both shares, red's the rest, read off the two whole-number shares: a split of 100. The name is the board's; in probability the odds of p are p / (1 − p), and this is neither those nor a fitted probability:

odds_blue = round( 100 · b / (b + r) )      odds_red = 100 − odds_blue
            b, r: blue's and red's shares; round: to the nearest whole number, a half to the even one

A seat still drafting is read through its fill, the best six its picks can reach, on both sides alike: a sum over fewer picks reads low, so the seat with more picks would lead. Where no fill is solved - red's revealed picks already past a limit, or a fill past the search's budget - the seat is read off its picks as they stand, and its badge says so. There are no odds where a seat has no picks, is unscored or not allowed, or where both shares are 0: each bar shows its own seat's share, an unscored or not-allowed seat its badge's word.

The problem's class. With V the heroes the board leaves open - released and unbanned - a six is x ∈ {0,1}^V, x_h = 1 where h is picked, and the search solves

maximise    score(x)
subject to  Σ_h x_h = 6
            x_h = 1                  for each locked pick h
            Σ_(h a tank) x_h ≤ %(MAX_TANKS)d
            each shape limit         linear in the role counts; the shipped one is
                                     Σ_(h a support) x_h ≤ 3
            each other limit         a predicate on x's facts; none is shipped

It is a nonlinear 0-1 program. base(x) is a quadratic pseudo-Boolean function: the rate and counter terms are linear in x - a six always holds six picks, and the other side is fixed for the board - and the synergy term is Σ_(h<k) S_hk x_h x_k with S symmetric and nonnegative, so base is supermodular: what a pick adds never falls as the picks beside it grow. The playbook's terms are not quadratic. They read counts and sums over the picks, the largest or smallest of a value among them (range_max, range_min), clamps, a ratio - the healing floor's shortfall, piecewise linear-fractional in H_x and P_x - and guards that switch a term on or off with x. So the objective is neither linear nor quadratic, and convexity does not apply: the set is discrete, and no continuous relaxation is solved. Exactness needs none of these: the set is finite, and the search drops only what a valid bound proves cannot place.

The argmax. The search is exact or refuses: past its budget of branches walked or sixes scored it answers nothing, never a six it has not proved, and a search that ends with no six proves that none is legal. Every legal shape is filled role by role from every released, unbanned hero of each role, each six built once. The search keeps the best few sixes - the optimal and the alternatives after it - and drops a branch only where a bound proves that no six in it can enter them: the bound, rounded as the scores are, falls below the last kept six's score, or meets it while the largest tie-break the branch can hold falls below that six's.

bound(branch) = base over the picks made, exactly
              + each open role's best n candidates by
                  own(h) + pairs(h, the picks made) + half the sum of h's m − 1 best pairs
                  n the role's open slots, m all the open slots, a pair with any other candidate
              + each playbook term at its best over an interval that holds every value its
                metrics take over the branch, its range or wider:
                  a heuristic at its norm's better end, a need at 0 where its guard may not hold,
                  a scored term at w · ( bonus's high end − penalty's low end ),
                  0 where its when may not hold
own(h)        = h's rate and counter part, and in the swap search its keep bonus

A limit the intervals prove false on every six of a branch drops the branch too; one they cannot settle drops each six that breaks it as the walk reaches it. Sixes rank by their score to %(SCORE_PLACES)d decimal places, rounded, so two scores that round alike tie; then by their tie-break, the sum of their heroes' draws on this board, the larger first; then by their sorted names, alphabetically first. A full six's rank is its place in that order - one that ties the best six's score and loses the tie-break is not first - counted exactly up to %(RANK_CAP)d.

What is searched. Every legal six: 17,217,564 on an open board under the shipped playbook at the roster of 2026-09-28, 53 released heroes, at most %(MAX_TANKS)d tanks and three supports; a hero released or a limit changed moves the count. The search scores a few dozen of them in full and proves the rest cannot beat them, so the answer is the best of all of them, and the alternatives are the next best in order. The suite checks it on every run: on small boards of a twelve-hero test roster, with bans, locked picks, red revealed and a pair that pays only together, under the reference playbook with a two-two-two role queue and with the open queue's shapes, and on two boards of that roster widened to seven a role under the role queue, the default engine on and off and the swap search's keep term with it, the search returns exactly the sixes a full enumeration ranks first. On the built database, a brute force of every legal six on real boards agreed with it six for six, score for score.

What it is optimal against. A comp is optimal against the red you can see, not against every red they might pick. Before red reveals anything the board answers their likely starting comp, filled from the map's pick rates; each pick they show re-solves against what is now known. It is a best reply to present information and changes as that information does. The hedge is the if countered optimally case the board tool reports: it scores your six against red's best reply to it, so a comp that wins only while red plays badly shows up there. The board page does not solve it.

Swaps and stages. A six is not held for the map. Above blue's picks the board suggests the swaps that pay for their cost, as one answer: of every legal six, the one that scores most less the swap cost for each of blue's picks it drops, a tie to the board's draw. The swaps are that one six, so none pulls against another: no hero comes in twice and no slot takes two. Taking one leaves that six the search's best from the new picks:

net_R(x)  = score(x) − c · |R − x|        R: blue's picks; c: the cost in the score's points
R'        = R less the pick p dropped, plus the hero q taken
net_R'(x) = net_R(x) + c · ( [p ∉ x] − [q ∉ x] )      [·]: 1 where it holds, else 0
          ≤ net_R(target) + c = net_R'(target)

A six that reaches the bound tied the target from R and loses the same draw. The board reads the rest from the new picks as it reads any swap, so it can still keep the picks: on a tie with them, or where the rest would not raise the fight odds. The cost is in share points of blue's span - c = swap · (best − floor) / 100 in the score's points - %(SWAP)g by default (meta.md's swap, 0 to %(SWAP_MAX)g, a slider on the playbook tab), and stands in for the ultimate charge and the walk a swap costs. The gain is read on the scores, so the whole, clamped shares the verdict shows can differ from it by a point, and by more where the picks score below the floor. Where red has revealed picks, a swap that would not raise blue's fight odds is withheld; where there are no odds to read, before or after, the net decides alone. Red is never told to swap.

The plan stage by stage walks the map's stages in play order from the six the comps tab shows - blue's picks at six, the fill around fewer, the optimal before any pick; the board's chosen stage plays the swaps suggested above the picks, the phases before it read as played, and the phases after it go on from its six. Each phase of a route is the best six reachable from the phase before, net of the cost for each hero changed, one stage at a time: its heroes stay unless the swaps there gain more than their cost, or the six before breaks the stage's limits. Each arena of a Control or Flashpoint map is reached from the comps tab's six, since the game sets no order. An unscored seat walks its stages at no cost. A stage differs from its map through the ground in play - its terrain, what the wiki's text on it stresses, its name, and on a Hybrid its objective, the first phase a point and the rest payload - and the playbook's rules that read them; the win rates are per map.

Reach. The weights are meant to make a hero common or rare, never impossible. What is established is narrower: at the reach fixture's recording of 2026-09-30, under the shipped playbook - the default engine, the healing floor and the terrain rules - 50 of the 53 released heroes had a board on which they are in the optimal six infer solves, within a match's %(MAX_BANS)d bans, and each such board is a proof: the six is the exact optimum. Where such a board leaves red unrevealed, infer reads red as no picks, while the board page reads red's likely six in every term and may seat another six. The search found none for three: Emre, Freja and Sojourn; a roster, a playbook or a weight that moves re-records it. That is not evidence that no such board exists, only that a search over every map but a few reds did not find one. No rule forbids any of them; the engine does not value what they do on the boards it tried, and the playbook's rules are what can answer that.

When nothing scores - the meta at 0 and no term of the playbook scoring - every legal six ties at zero, the board reads unscored, and the optimal is the draw's pick among them, marked as one of the sixes tied at the best score.

Deterministic, not probabilistic. The engine computes; it does not sample. The same board, playbook and weights give the same six, the same score and the same alternatives in any process, and on any day the data holds still: nothing is left to chance when a board is solved - its one draw, the reference sample, is pseudo-random from a string seed and the same every time. The weights are inputs like the picks: move the Meta slider or a rule's and the objective moves, so the six may too; move it back and the same six returns. The reference sample and the tie-break's draws come from string seeds, the board's map and side; the scale's field reads the other side's picks besides. What reads like chance is a tie or a share. A tie is several sixes whose scores round to the same %(SCORE_PLACES)d decimal places, none of them better; the board's draws - each hero's a hash of the map, the side and the hero's id, blind to its rates and its name - pick the one shown, the six whose draws sum highest, and the board says how many tie. With the meta at 0 and every weight at 0, every legal six ties: each hero of a role has the same chance of being the one drawn across boards, and one board draws the same six every time. A share is a score's place on its seat's span and the fight odds a ratio of two shares, not probabilities of winning: nothing here is fitted to match results. The playbook holds both as assumptions, Tied sixes are equally good and Deterministic, not probabilistic.

The board

Red's likely starting comp: a two-two-two filled slot by slot, past the bans, each slot the open-role hero of the highest pull below - a greedy fill by a score in pick-rate points, not the six a probability ranks first. No strategy is read, and it does not change when red reveals a pick.

pull(h) = pick(h, map) + %(SYNERGY_PULL)g × partners(h, the six so far)

pick(h, map)   the hero's pick rate on this map; overall with no map set or no rate here, 0 with none
partners(h, S) how many heroes already on S the synergy table pairs h with
               a tie goes to the name first in alphabetical order

Blue's optimal counter: solved against red's revealed picks, or their likely comp until they reveal one, on this map, side and bans; never against blue's own picks. Once blue picks, its seat shows blue's six above the optimal: from one to five picks, the picks with the rest filled by a second solve that keeps them - the six the suggestion tiles offer; at six, the six itself. The game plan describes that six. Red's optimal is solved as the scale for red's score and not shown.

The weights. A heuristic's weight is the slider under its card. It stays in your browser and rides with every request until reset; the file's weight is the default, and only the tune tool changes it. Every heuristic has a slider, 0 to %(WEIGHT_MAX)g, on a metric or scored; a constraint is never weighted. The Meta slider, on the meta's card at the head of the playbook tab, does the same for meta.md's meta: the whole default engine, scaled for your session.

How the pieces fit

sources → db/data (fetch) → db/psql (the database) → facts/ (FACTS of a board)
                                                                  ↓
                   inference/strategies (STRATEGIES) → inference/solver (ARGMAX) → the board

The data layer (db/): the sources in one Postgres schema.

The facts layer (facts/): the World, the metrics registry and the FactSet - every table turned into numbered facts, each metric defined once for the board and the solver alike.

The inference layer (inference/): the playbook and the solver. Expressions are compiled once and whitelisted. The solver runs no model and opens no connection. No code in the layer calls a language model.

The door (door/): the MCP tools, the one way in for a write, and the clock that runs them daily.

The board (ui/): the facts layer's facts of one board and the inference layer's answer at each stage of a draft; serve.py reads the World per request. It never calls a language model.

Claude: runs when you ask. It refreshes the sources, derives a strategy's mathematics from its prose, and tunes weights. /comp is the same solver in chat; with no red given, only the default engine's counter term reads red's likely six.
