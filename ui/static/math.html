The Counter Utility Matrix

Countrix is short for Counter Utility Matrix, the equation below. The counter is who answers whom: which hero counters which. The utility is what a six - one team of six heroes - is worth once the facts are counted: one number for the whole six, not a sum of its picks' worths, since a pair can pay only together. The matrix is every row of the data crossed with every other: heroes against heroes, heroes against maps, and both against META, Blizzard's rates. A comp, a team composition, is the argmax of the utility the matrix feeds: the legal six that scores highest.

DATA           = HEROES ∪ MAPS ∪ META
for each domain D in { HEROES, MAPS, META }:
  INDEPENDENT(D) = ⋃ facts(s)      over each row s of D
  DEPENDENT(D)   = ⋃ facts(s ⋈ t)  over the rows t that s joins, in D or beyond
  FACTS(D)       = INDEPENDENT(D) ∪ DEPENDENT(D)
FACTS          = FACTS(HEROES) ∪ FACTS(MAPS) ∪ FACTS(META)
FACTS(D) ∩ FACTS(E) = the joins of D with E, read as facts, for D ≠ E
STRATEGIES     = CONSTRAINTS ∪ HEURISTICS ∪ ASSUMPTIONS
COMP           = ARGMAX[ STRATEGIES( FACTS ) ]
               = the first six x of LEGAL by score(x)
LEGAL          = the sixes of released, unbanned heroes that keep the locked picks,
                 at most %(MAX_TANKS)d tanks, every CONSTRAINT true on FACTS(x)
score(x)       = base(x) + each HEURISTIC's term on FACTS(x); an ASSUMPTION adds nothing
FACTS(x)       = the board's FACTS, plus the six's team and matchup facts, computed on x
first          = the highest score to %(SCORE_PLACES)d places, then the larger tie-break - the sum
                 of the heroes' hash numbers (see the argmax) - then the names

DATA: the database's tables, fetched from Blizzard and the wiki. HEROES: kits, their published numbers, playstyles, counters, synergies. MAPS: mode, stages, sides, and the terrain the wiki's articles describe, counted as mentions per thousand words (chokes, interiors, high ground, flanks, sightlines, open ground, hazards, cover). META: win, pick and ban rates per hero and per map, dated.

FACTS: what the facts layer derives for one board and one six. A board is one draft: the map, its stage where one is chosen, blue's side, the bans and both teams' picks so far. Blue is your team, the one Countrix picks a six for; red is the other. The board's facts are read once; the six's team and matchup facts are computed again for every six scored. The independent facts of a domain come from one row: a hero's health pool, range and rates; a map's mode, stages and terrain; the date the rates were captured. Each terrain feature is standardised across the maps whose article has text: its distance from the maps' average, counted in standard deviations, a standard deviation being the typical distance of a value from the average.

The dependent facts are joins (⋈), facts that read two or more tables at once. A hero's rate on this map is heroes ⋈ map_meta ⋈ maps. A map's style - brawl, dive or poke - is the one with the largest sum of two parts, each standardised across the maps, which the facts tab shows as rates and terrain. The rates part is playstyle ⋈ map_meta: how many win-rate points more the style's heroes win on this map than overall, on average, each hero weighted 1 over its number of playstyle tags. The terrain part is how much the map's article stresses the terrain that suits the style - chokes and interiors for brawl; high ground, flanks and hazards for dive; sightlines and open ground for poke - the average of those standardised features. The rest are who a hero answers (heroes ⋈ counters ⋈ heroes), who it pairs with, the six's totals (team facts), the six against the other side's picks (matchup facts), and each ban weighed against both teams' counters. Facts are numbered F1, F2, …; every claim on the board page cites one.

STRATEGIES: the playbook, one markdown file per strategy. Constraints cut the space, then heuristics weigh what is left. A constraint is a limit: it always holds and is never weighted. A heuristic is either on a metric - a metric, a direction (maximise or minimise) and a weight - or scored: two expressions, a bonus and a penalty, and it adds (bonus − penalty) × weight. Either may count only under a condition, its when. A limit still reaches the scores in one way: the sixes that set each heuristic's scale (the function) are only sixes the limits allow, so adding or dropping a limit rescales every heuristic on a metric, and can move the optimal six even when that six keeps the limit. An assumption is prose, shown on the playbook tab; it adds nothing to a score. Read as a function, STRATEGIES( FACTS ) is score(x) on the legal sixes: the constraints set its domain, the heuristics its terms, over the default engine's base(x), whose weights meta.md holds.

How a six is chosen

Inference on maxims and facts. The maxims are the playbook's rules, the facts the database's numbers about this board, and together they make one objective, weighted and constrained, which the search solves exactly or refuses. Five steps, in order:

1  the space           every six of released, unbanned heroes that keeps the locked picks,
                       each counted once, grouped tank, damage, support; at most %(MAX_TANKS)d tanks
2  the limits          each constraint removes the sixes that break it, and weighs nothing
3  the default engine  scores every six left, times the engine weight (meta in meta.md):
                       each pick's win-rate edge trusted by its pick rate, synergies, counters
4  the heuristics      each adds or subtracts, times its weight, from the same facts
5  the argmax          the best legal six, proved, then the next best in order;
                       every reason cites a fact

An open board - no bans and no picks - held about 13.0 million legal sixes under the shipped playbook of 2026-10-03 at the roster of 2026-09-28 (what is searched); a ban or a locked pick leaves fewer. A synergy pair no article writes about is unknown, not zero; synergy(x) in the function says what it counts. The weights are not learned. A rule's starting weight comes from its prose on the house scale - 0.25 a whisper, 1 the default, 2.5 strong, 4 dominant - or from the owner's word, as heal-rate's 2 did. Each weight is yours to turn: the tune tool for the files, a slider for a session. A score is not a probability. Blue's badge, the number above blue's picks, places blue's score between the share floor, 0, and blue's best six on the board, 100 (what 100 means). Red is never optimized: Countrix reads red's likely six instead (red's likely comp). The rates are Competitive Role Queue, 5v5, a proxy for Open Queue 6v6, so a value a hero has only beside a second tank, such as Zarya's in a two-tank front line, is missing from them.

The function: STRATEGIES( FACTS )

One six in, one number out: score(x), the default engine's base(x) with the playbook's heuristics on top; the constraints decide which sixes it is read on. The facts are its namespace, the facts tab's own numbers: team, the six itself; enemy, red's revealed picks; matchup, the six against them; map, the ground in play; and world, roster-wide benchmarks.

The default engine scores every six before the playbook reads it, whatever the playbook holds. Its rate term reads the six's win rates on this map: each pick's win rate counts as its edge over a coin flip, %(COIN_FLIP)g, trusted by its pick rate, so a hero picked in few matches counts for less. Its synergy term reads the wiki's synergy pairs among the picks. Its counter term reads the counter graph between the six and the other side: the wiki's edges, from its Match-Up tables and its Strategy sections, and an edge the kits supply where the wiki has none between two heroes either way. %(MECHANISMS)d mechanisms, from hitscan against a flier to anti-heal against self-healing, read two heroes' kits and score every ordered pair. For each hero, its %(TOP_ANSWERS)d best answers that score %(THRESHOLD)g or more, and more than the reverse pair, are candidates; a candidate on a pair the wiki covers is dropped and not replaced, and the rest are derived edges. A wiki edge counts %(WIKI_WEIGHT)d, a derived one %(DERIVED_WEIGHT)d, and the board page names every derived edge it counts with the mechanism that fired. Only this term reads a derived edge - the playbook's counter metrics read the wiki's graph alone.

The other side is six heroes: red's revealed picks and, for the rest, its likeliest heroes, each open slot taking the hero with the highest pick score - how often a six fields the hero, plus a bonus for each synergy partner already chosen (red's likely comp). That is red's likely six before any pick, and its picks alone once it holds six. Each reveal is kept and the rest refilled around it, so the term reads six heroes throughout, the count its weight was calibrated on. Of the objective's terms only this one reads the likely heroes; the fight odds read them too. Every other term that reads red - the healing bar, the enemy.* and matchup.* metrics, the counter metrics - reads only red's revealed picks, none before a reveal, on the board page and in infer alike. A playbook of assumptions alone gets the six the default engine's rate, synergy and counter terms favour, scored and explained, and the playbook's terms sit on top of them.

A limit that fails removes the six. A heuristic on a metric is read against %(REFERENCE_SIZE)s random sixes of a legal shape (a six's shape is its count of tanks, damage and supports), the reference sample, and against the board's top-hero sixes (the code's field). Its norm is its metric's value placed on 0 to 1 between the lowest and highest values the metric takes over those sixes, and flipped when the rule minimises. Each six of the sample is drawn shape first, each shape the limits allow equally likely, then each role's heroes from every released hero, by a string seed of the map and the side alone, so red's picks, the bans and blue's locked picks never move it. The top-hero sixes are every six of a legal shape built from each role's %(SCALE_POOL)d released heroes that rank highest on this board by a point score: the win rate here (overall where the map has none), plus 3 for each of red's revealed picks the wiki says the hero answers, less 3 for each that answers it, plus 1 where the map's style is among the hero's playstyles and 1 where this is one of its three best maps by win rate. That ranking reads red's revealed picks, never the bans or blue's locked picks. Without these sixes a strong six would sit above the random sample's top on most metrics, where its norm stops at 1 and a rule cannot tell two strong sixes apart. Both sets are measured on the whole map, and a six a limit refuses is left out of both.

A heuristic's condition, its when, is settled by the board when it reads only enemy.*, map.*, world.* and its params, and decided by the six when it reads team.* or matchup.*. A heuristic on a metric with no condition, or with one the board settles, is a reward: it adds its weight times its norm. One whose condition the six decides is a need: it counts only on a six that meets the condition, costs nothing when its norm is 1 and at most its weight otherwise, so meeting the condition never pays. For example, dive-moves-together counts only on a six whose leading playstyle is dive (team.style_lean): of the playstyles more than half the picks carry, the one most picks carry, a tie going to the map's style, else to the first in alphabetical order. A need's guard is its condition with its params. A scored heuristic is never a need: while its condition holds, on the board or on the six, it adds (bonus − penalty) × weight, so a six can gain by meeting a condition it decides. To make a rule cost points instead of forbidding a six, write it as a scored heuristic with when: not (the rule) and a penalty.

for one six x, with every limit holding:

           a term whose condition fails adds 0

The engine's weights. They live in meta.md beside the strategy files, and the tune tool changes them. The engine weight, meta in meta.md, is %(W_META)g: it scales the whole default engine, and at 0 the playbook scores alone. Under it the rate term is in win-rate points and weighs %(W_RATE)g. The synergy and counter weights are set so that each term's spread - its highest value less its lowest over a board's reference sample, with red's likely six as the other side - is about half the rate term's, as a median over the maps; the calibration is in inference/base.py. The comparisons below are at engine weight 1 and the shipped weights, each spread taken as its median over the maps; under another playbook the weights are its own meta.md's, and the comparisons do not carry over. A heuristic on a metric moves a six by its weight at most, since its norm is 0 to 1 and s_n is 1 at most. A scored one moves it by its weight times (bonus − penalty), which its own expressions bound. Every shipped scored rule keeps its bonus and its penalty within 0 to 1 inside the shipped limits, so it too moves a six by its weight at most. two-of-each-role counts each pick past two in a role, and one more for a six a tank short, and divides by 3, the most the limits allow, which a one-tank, four-damage six reaches. edges-reward-displacement counts the picks past the second with a tool that moves an enemy, up to its cap of 3, and divides by that cap. A base term has no such bound. These comparisons hold for any rule whose weight bounds its move, and every shipped rule's does: at weight 1 such a rule moves a six less than any base term's spread, at 2 about as far as the synergy or the counter term's, and at the slider's %(WEIGHT_MAX)g further than the three together.

Sustained healing. A hero's hps (healing per second) is the healing it lands on teammates each second: its best healing weapon and every ability and passive it can use beside that weapon, ultimates aside, each counted over every teammate it reaches. Healing on itself does not count. A beam that spends energy counts what the energy sustains: fire, wait out the regeneration delay, regenerate, at the cycle that heals most. The six stand at random points of a disk of radius R = %(FORMATION_RADIUS)g m, every point equally likely - the formation - R being the shortest single-target heal range among the supports, and p(r) is the chance two of them stand within r of each other:

The healing bar. The shipped playbook's healing heuristic, heal-rate, a scored rule, charges a six that heals less than a bar set by the other side's healing. The bar comes from a race. A six has a pool P (health, shield, armor), damage D and sustained healing onto teammates H. Each side's damage leaves the same share k of the other's healing landing, 0 < k ≤ 1, so x loses (D_r − k H_x) / P_x of its pool a second and red (D_x − k H_r) / P_r, and the one that loses the smaller share outlasts the other. The margin, (D_x / P_r − D_r / P_x) + k (H_x / P_x − H_r / P_r), splits into a damage part and a healing part, and the healing part breaks even when blue heals the same share of its own pool as red heals of its own, whatever k is. The bar is that share of blue's pool, and never less than red's healing in full. That lower limit is a design choice the race does not imply: parity alone would ask less of a six with a smaller pool than red's, so a six could lower its bar by having less health. Above red's pool, the bar still grows with the pool.

heal_need(x) = max( H_r, H_r / P_r · P_x ), H_r where P_r is 0
shortfall(x) = max( 0, heal_need(x) − H_x ) / heal_need(x)      0 when heal_need(x) is 0
charge(x)    = w · shortfall(x)      w the rule's weight in the playbook

H_x, P_x     x's summed sustained healing onto teammates, and its pool with a form's armor
             counted by its uptime
H_r          red's healing  + f · d_support · hps_bench / 2
P_r          red's pool     + f · Σ_q d_q · median pool of role q
d_q          max( 0, 2 − red's picks in role q ): what the two-two-two still misses
f            red's open slots / Σ_q d_q, 0 when nothing is missing

Red here is red's revealed picks, their open slots filled as above; the likely six is not read. Before red reveals a pick, red is a two-two-two of role-median heroes: it heals world.hps_bench, the benchmark - twice the median support's healing - and its pool is twice the sum of the role-median pools in the kit in force. A six must then heal that share of its own pool each second, and never less than the benchmark, and everything in that bar is kit data. The sources give no absolute bar - no fight length, no ultimate charge - so the rule asks for parity with the other side's healing and no more: at parity the healing part of the margin is zero and damage decides the race, and healing past the bar earns nothing from the rule. The bar has three caveats, read on the 6v6 kit as of 2026-09-26. First, the benchmark is fragile: Illari, Mizuki and Moira sit within 0.7 hp/s of each other around the median support, each figure resting on a modelling judgement; the benchmark moves one for one with the two supports that set the median, and if one of them were rated above Kiriko it would rise to about 151 hp/s. Second, overhealing is not subtracted, so an untargeted area heal, which also lands on full-health teammates, reads above what it heals. Third, hps takes Brigitte's Flail to be in contact throughout; at half contact she reads 61.7 hp/s in place of 98.4.

What 100 means. Blue's best six on this board is its optimal, 100. Its 0 is the share floor: the lowest score among the reference sixes that keep the board's limits, each scored with the board's stage in force. The sample is drawn from every released hero whatever the bans, so the floor six may hold a banned one; the top-hero sixes do not enter the floor. Where no reference six keeps the limits, a score of 0 stands in as the floor, though such a score means nothing in particular (below); under the shipped limits, which test only the six's shape, every reference six keeps them. Under the shipped playbook of 2026-10-04, on every map, side and stage with nothing picked or banned, the floor six was a one-tank, four-damage six, the one shape two-of-each-role charges in full, so that rule's weight moves the 0, and with it every share and the swap cost. Scores can be negative - the rate term counts each pick's edge over a coin flip - so a score of 0 means nothing in particular, and the share floor serves as the 0 instead. The badge above blue's picks places blue's six between the two - its picks at six, the best six its picks reach while it drafts, the optimal's 100 before any pick - under the default engine and this playbook, not a win probability:

share(x) = round( clamp( (score(x) − floor) / (best − floor), 0, 1 ) · 100 )
           best: blue's optimal six's score; floor: the share floor
           round: to the nearest whole number, a half to the even one

If blue's optimal scores no higher than the share floor, there is no range to divide, so every other comp reads unscored, and the reason is on hover. Blue's picks are not allowed when a full six breaks a limit, or when no six that keeps them meets the limits. Such a comp gets no score and no share, and its badge names the limits the picks break, or says that no six keeping them meets the limits. Red's picks are never ruled out. While blue drafts, its badge reads its fill: its picks with the empty slots filled by the best six that keeps them, since a sum over fewer picks reads low. Where no fill is solved, because the fill's search ran past its budget, blue is read off its picks as they stand, and its badge says so.

Fight odds. The fight odds split 100 between blue's six and red's likely six by how far apart their scores are. They compare two scores and are not a chance of winning, and the strip above the teams shows them without a percent sign. Blue's six is the six the game plan describes. Both sixes are scored on one scale: the default engine alone, against red's likely six. No playbook rule scores either six, so both are judged alike. Read against itself, red's six counts no counter, so each counter between the two sixes counts once, in blue's score. The gap between the two scores, counted in standard deviations of a random six's score, goes through a logistic curve, an S-shaped curve that turns any gap into a number between 0 and 100:

odds(blue) = round( 100 / (1 + e^(−(s(blue) − s(red)) / σ)) ),   odds(red) = 100 − odds(blue)
           s: the default engine's score against red's likely six
           σ: the standard deviation of s over the board's reference sample

A gap of one σ reads 73 to 27, equal scores read 50 to 50, and swapping the two scores swaps the split; scaling or shifting every score alike leaves it where it is. The playbook chooses blue's six but scores neither; it reaches the scale only through σ, taken over the same draw of reference sixes that scales blue's heuristics, drawn over the shapes the playbook's limits allow. With the engine off, with a sample whose scores are all equal, with blue's picks not allowed, or with blue half-drafted and no fill solved, there are no odds. Blue's six is chosen to score well against red's likely six, while red's is chosen by pick score, so blue usually leads by far.

The problem's class. With V the heroes the board leaves open - released and unbanned - a six is x ∈ {0,1}^V, x_h = 1 where h is picked, and the search solves

maximise    score(x)
subject to  Σ_h x_h = 6
            x_h = 1                  for each locked pick h
            Σ_(h a tank) x_h ≤ %(MAX_TANKS)d
            each shape limit         a test on the role counts alone; the shipped ones are
                                     Σ_(h a tank) x_h ≥ 1 and 1 ≤ Σ_(h a support) x_h ≤ 3
            each other limit         a true-or-false test on x's facts; none is shipped

It is a nonlinear 0-1 program: an optimisation over yes-or-no choices, one per hero. base(x) is a quadratic pseudo-Boolean function - a number computed from those yes-or-no choices, with terms for single heroes and for pairs. The rate and counter terms are linear in x - a six always holds six picks, and the other side is fixed for the board - and the synergy term is Σ_(h<k) S_hk x_h x_k with S symmetric and nonnegative, so base is supermodular: what a pick adds never falls as the picks beside it grow. The playbook's terms are not quadratic. They read counts and sums over the picks, the median of a value over them (range_median), the largest of several sums (style_share, the biggest playstyle's share of the picks, a pick with k playstyles giving 1/k to each), clamps, and ratios - the supports' mean healing (hps_per_support) and the healing bar's shortfall, piecewise linear-fractional in H_x and P_x, a ratio of linear expressions taken in pieces - and conditions that switch a term on or off with x. So the objective is neither linear nor quadratic, and convexity, the property that makes many continuous problems easy, does not apply: the set is discrete, and no continuous relaxation (a version that allows fractional picks) is solved. Exactness rests on none of these: the set is finite, and the search drops only what a valid bound proves cannot reach the kept list.

The argmax. The search is exact or refuses. It walks the sixes as a tree, one pick at a time; a branch is every six that shares the picks made so far. Every legal shape is filled role by role from every released, unbanned hero of each role, each six built once. The search keeps the best few sixes - the optimal and the alternatives after it - and drops a branch only where its bound, a score no six in the branch can beat, proves that no six in it can enter them: the bound, rounded as the scores are, falls below the last kept six's score, or meets it while the largest tie-break the branch can hold falls below that six's. Past its budget of branches walked or sixes scored, it returns no answer rather than a six it has not proved, and a search that ends with no six proves that none is legal.

bound(branch) = base over the picks made, exactly
              + each open role's best n candidates by
                  own(h) + pairs(h, the picks made) + half the sum of h's m − 1 best pairs
                  n the role's open slots, m all the open slots, a pair with any other candidate
              + each playbook term at its best over an interval that holds every value its
                metrics take over the branch, its range or wider:
                  a heuristic at its norm's better end, a need at 0 where its guard may not hold,
                  a scored term at w · ( bonus's high end − penalty's low end ),
                  the larger of that and 0 where its condition may not hold
own(h)        = h's rate and counter part; in the swap search, plus c where h is one of
                blue's picks - the keep term (swaps and stages)

A limit the intervals prove false on every six of a branch drops the branch too; one they cannot settle drops each six that breaks it as the walk reaches it. Sixes rank by their score to %(SCORE_PLACES)d decimal places, rounded, so two scores that round alike tie; then by their tie-break, the sum of their heroes' tie-break numbers, the larger first, where each hero's tie-break number is a hash of the map, the side and the hero's id; then by their sorted names, alphabetically first. A full six's rank is its place in that order - one that ties the best six's score and loses the tie-break is not first - counted exactly up to %(RANK_CAP)d.

What is searched. Every legal six: 13,030,920 on an open board under the shipped playbook of 2026-10-03 at the roster of 2026-09-28, 53 released heroes, 1 to %(MAX_TANKS)d tanks and 1 to 3 supports; a hero released or a limit changed moves the count. The search scores at most a few hundred of them in full and proves the rest cannot beat them, so the answer is the best of all of them, and the alternatives are the next best in order. The suite checks the search against a full enumeration on every run, on small boards of a twelve-hero test roster: with bans, locked picks, red revealed, and a pair that pays only together; under the test playbook in tests/fixtures/playbook, both with a two-two-two role queue and with the open queue's shapes; on two boards with the roster widened to seven heroes a role, under the role queue; with the default engine on and off; and with the keep term. On every board the search returns exactly the sixes the enumeration ranks first. On the built database, a brute force of every legal six on real boards matched it on every six and every score.

What it is optimal against. A comp is optimal against red as the board stands: red's likely six in the default engine's counter term, and red's revealed picks everywhere else. As red reveals picks, the likely six is rebuilt around them, and the search runs again against what is now known. It is a best reply to present information, and it changes as that information does. Red is never solved, so nothing scores your six against red's best reply to it.

Swaps and stages. Blue can swap heroes during a map. Above blue's picks the board page suggests the swaps that pay for their cost, as one answer: of every legal six, the one that scores most less the swap cost for each of blue's picks it drops, a tie going to the tie-break. The suggested swaps all lead to that one six, so no two conflict: no hero comes in twice and no slot takes two. Taking one leaves that six the best answer from the new picks:

net_R(x)  = score(x) − c · |R − x|        R: blue's picks; c: the cost in the score's points
R'        = R less the pick p dropped, plus the hero q taken
net_R'(x) = net_R(x) + c · ( [p ∉ x] − [q ∉ x] )      [·]: 1 where it holds, else 0
          ≤ net_R(target) + c = net_R'(target)

Only a six that tied the target from R can reach that bound, and it loses the same tie-break, so the target stays first. The search adds c for each of blue's picks a six keeps - the keep term - which ranks every six as its net does. A swap is suggested only where the target's net beats the six that keeps every pick, blue's picks at six or their fill with fewer; on a tie no swap is suggested. The cost is set in share points, points of blue's 0 to 100 share, so c = swap · (best − share floor) / 100 in the score's points; it is %(SWAP)g share points by default (swap in meta.md, 0 to %(SWAP_MAX)g, a slider on the playbook tab), and stands in for the ultimate charge and the walk back to spawn that a swap costs. The gain is read on the raw scores, so the rounded, clamped shares the verdict shows can differ from it by a point, and by more where the picks score below the share floor.

The stage plan walks the map's stages in play order, starting from the six the comps tab shows: blue's picks at six, their fill with one to five, the optimal with none. The stages of an Escort or Hybrid map are phases of one route, played in order: each phase is the best six reachable from the phase before, net of the cost for each hero changed, one phase at a time, so its heroes stay unless the swaps there gain more than their cost, or the six before breaks the stage's limits. The stages of a Control or Flashpoint map are played in no fixed order, so each is reached from the comps tab's six. A stage chosen on the board page plays the swaps suggested above the picks; the phases before it count as played, and the phases after it go on from its six. When blue is unscored, the plan swaps at no cost. A stage differs from its map only through the ground in play - its terrain, what the wiki's text on it stresses, its name, and on a Hybrid its objective, the first phase a point and the rest payload - and the playbook's rules that read them; the win rates are per map.

Reach. The weights are meant to make a hero common or rare, never impossible. What has been measured is narrower. When the reach fixture was recorded on 2026-10-03, under the playbook and the default engine of that recording, 50 of the 53 released heroes had a board on which they are in the optimal six, within a match's %(MAX_BANS)d bans. Each such board is a proof: the six is the exact optimum, on the board page and in infer alike. The search found none for Emre, Freja and Sojourn. It tried every map but only a few red line-ups, so they may still be optimal on a board it did not try. No rule forbids them, and a playbook rule could raise their value. A roster, a playbook or a weight that moves means recording it again.

When nothing scores - the engine weight at 0 and no playbook term scoring - every legal six ties at zero, blue's badge reads unscored, and the optimal is the tie-break's pick among them, marked as one of the sixes tied at the best score.

Deterministic, not probabilistic. Given the same data, board, playbook and weights, every run returns the same six, score and alternatives, in any process. The one random draw, the reference sample, comes from a fixed string seed, the map and the side, and is the same every time. The tie-break numbers depend on the map and the side alone, and the top-hero sixes on the board's map and red's revealed picks. The weights are inputs like the picks: move a slider and the objective moves, so the six may too; move it back and the same six returns. What reads like chance is a tie, a share or the fight odds. A tie is several sixes whose scores round alike (the argmax), none of them better; the tie-break picks the one shown, and the board page says how many tie. A tie-break number is blind to the hero's rates and name, so when every legal six ties, each hero of a role is equally likely to be the one shown across boards, and one board shows the same six every time. A share is a score's place between the share floor and blue's best six, and the fight odds a curve on the gap between two scores; neither is a chance of winning. Red's pick score ranks heroes by how often a six fields them and by their documented synergies. Nothing here is learned from match results; the rates enter only as data. The playbook holds both points as assumptions, Tied sixes are equally good and Deterministic, not probabilistic.

The board page

Red's likely comp: red's revealed picks, then, one slot at a time until there are six, the hero with the highest pick score among the released, unbanned heroes not yet chosen whose role the two-two-two still lacks. It is a greedy fill - each slot takes the best hero left, and no slot is revisited - so the six as a whole need not have the highest total pick score. Red is never optimized: no strategy is read for it, and only the fight odds score it, on the default engine alone. The board page suggests it in red's empty slots, red's badge says how often a six fields its heroes on average, and blue's counter term reads it.

pick score(h) = on_six(h) + %(PARTNER_POINTS)g × partners(h, the six so far)
on_six(h)     = min( 100, pick(h, map) × 2 / seats(h) ), in percent of sixes
pick(h, map)  the hero's published pick rate on this map: the share of 5v5 Role Queue teams that
              field it; overall with no map set or no rate here, 0 with none
seats(h)      a 5v5 Role Queue team's seats in h's role: 1 tank, 2 damage, 2 support - so a
              tank's rate doubles on a six's two tank seats
partners(h, S) how many heroes already on S the synergy table pairs h with; a revealed pick
              counts the revealed picks before it
              a tie goes to the name first in alphabetical order

Blue's optimal counter: the best legal six on this map, side and bans, against red as the objective reads it - red's likely six in the counter term, red's revealed picks everywhere else - and never constrained by blue's own picks. Once blue picks, blue's panel shows blue's six above the optimal: from one to five picks, its fill, the six the suggestion tiles offer; at six, the six itself. The game plan describes that six.

The weights. Each heuristic has a slider under its card on the playbook tab, 0 to %(WEIGHT_MAX)g, on a metric or scored. A slider's value stays in your browser and goes with every request until you reset it; the weight in the rule's file is the default, and only the tune tool changes the file. The Meta slider, on the playbook tab's first card, sets the engine weight for your session in the same way.

How the pieces fit

sources → db/data (fetch) → db/psql (the database) → facts/ (FACTS of a board)
                                                                  ↓
                   inference/strategies (STRATEGIES) → inference/solver (ARGMAX) → the board page

The data layer (db/): the sources in one Postgres schema.

The facts layer (facts/): the World - the whole database in memory - the metrics registry and the FactSet: every table turned into numbered facts, each metric defined once for the board page and the solver alike.

The inference layer (inference/): the playbook and the solver. Expressions are compiled once and whitelisted. No code in the layer calls a trained or language model, and the solver opens no database connection.

The door (door/): the MCP tools, the one way in for a write, and the clock that runs them daily.

The board page (ui/): the facts layer's facts of one board and the inference layer's answer at each stage of a draft; serve.py reads the World per request. It never calls a language model.

Claude: runs when you ask. It refreshes the sources, derives a strategy's mathematics from its prose, and tunes weights. /comp is the same solver in chat: the same board gives the same six there.
